An AI agent that works from Slack

Lore is an AI agent for Slack that turns the conversation into context for research, browser work, documents, checklists, and follow-ups, then reports the result back where the team is already talking. It works on one bounded mission at a time and uses approval gates for external actions.

The short answer

Lore works from the context in Slack. It can remember decisions, perform bounded work with connected tools, and return completed work to the same conversation. It uses approval gates for external actions.

Concrete capabilities

  • Recover the message behind a past decision.
  • Open public web pages in a real browser, read them, take screenshots, and return sourced work.
  • Create research briefs, documents, checklists, and follow-ups from what the chat decided.
  • Notice open asks and blockers, then prepare the next step.

How it works

  1. The team talks in Slack as usual.
  2. Lore uses the permitted conversation context and connected tools.
  3. Lore performs one bounded mission and records the evidence.
  4. The result comes back to the conversation. External sends, posts, bookings, and purchases require approval.

Limits and approval

Lore handles one mission at a time. It cannot use private sources or tools that a team has not connected and permitted. Availability and integrations can change. It prepares internal work, but actions that represent the team or spend money use approval gates.

Questions

What is Lore?

Lore is an AI teammate that lives in a team group chat. It uses the conversation as working context, remembers decisions, helps with open work, and reports results back in the same thread. The team can keep talking where it already works instead of rebuilding the request in another dashboard.

What can Lore do from Slack?

Lore can turn a message into bounded work such as research, browser tasks, documents, checklists, and follow-ups. When the required tools are connected and permitted, it performs that work and reports the result in the conversation. The team does not have to copy the original decision and its context into another app.

What requires approval?

External actions such as sending, posting, booking, or buying use approval gates. Lore can prepare internal work and show what it found first, but the team stays in control of actions that represent it or spend money. Permission is scoped to the specific action rather than assumed from access to the conversation.

What are the limits?

Lore handles one mission at a time. Integrations and availability can change, and it does not claim access to a tool or source unless that team has connected and permitted it. A browser or source can also be unavailable, so Lore reports the evidence it used and says when a task is blocked rather than inventing a result.
