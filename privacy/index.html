Privacy Policy

Effective October 28, 2026[EFFECTIVE DATE]

lore is an AI agent you text over iMessage, and an invite-only network of the people who use it. It remembers what you tell it so it can help you, and it can connect you with people you know, with your yes. This policy explains what lore collects, how its AI uses that information, who else handles it, how long it is kept, and the choices and rights you have. lore is for people 13 and older. We do not sell personal information, we do not use it for targeted advertising, and we do not train AI models on your content.

The short version

  • lore keeps your messages and what it learns from them so it can remember context and do what you ask.
  • What is said in a group stays with that group. What you tell lore one to one stays between you and lore.
  • To answer you, lore sends the text it needs to AI model providers and, for web tasks, to search and browser services. They handle it to provide their service to us.
  • lore is invite-only. When you join, lore's AI interviews you and decides whether you are in now or on the waitlist. You can ask a person to review it.
  • lore never introduces you, shares your contact details, or sends, posts, books or buys anything for you without your yes. or shares your contact details without your yes. It sends, posts, books or buys something for you only when you said yes, either in your request or when lore asks, or for a purchase within a spending limit you set.
  • You can download and delete your data. Say "lore forget me", "lore forget this group" or "forget me everywhere", or delete your account on your dashboard.
  • Nobody under 13 may use lore. People 13 to 17 get teen settings. A parent or guardian of a teen can ask us to delete their teen's data.

How you join

lore is invite-only. When you first text lore, it interviews you with a few questions about who you are, what you are working on and who you want to meet, and it may ask for a photo or screenshot of your work. To check what you tell it, lore may read links you give it and public web pages about you. lore's AI then decides whether to let you in now, put you on the waitlist, or ask more. You need a member's invite or vouch to get in; without one you go on the waitlist. lore asks everyone their age and does not let in anyone under 13.

You can ask a person at lore to review a decision by texting "talk to a human" or emailing [CONTACT EMAIL], and lore will look again when you have something new to show. If someone invited you, lore tells them when you are in, and may tell them kindly if you are not, but never shares your answers. We keep your interview until you are let in, you text STOP, or you ask us to delete it. If you are let in, lore uses it to draft your profile, which you approve before it goes live.

How lore's AI uses your information

lore is built on large language models. When a message reaches lore, the AI reads it together with the context lore keeps for that conversation (recent messages and the chat's memory and, in your own one-to-one chat, your personal profile and notes) and decides what you mean, whether to reply, and what to do. To do that, the text and any images it needs are sent to an AI model provider for that request.

  • No training on your content. We do not use your messages or memory to train AI models. When lore's requests go through OpenRouter, lore asks OpenRouter by default to use only model providers that do not store prompts or train on them. When lore uses a model provider directly, it uses that provider's terms that do not allow training on your content.
  • Outside content is information, not instructions. Web pages, emails and documents lore reads are treated as data. lore will not send anything just because a page or an email told it to.
  • Real actions need your yes. lore asks for your approval before it sends an email, posts, books, pays or buys anything for you.sends an email, posts, books, pays or buys something for you only when you said yes: in your request ("book it", "send it") or when lore shows you the step and asks. If you set a spending limit, lore can finish a purchase you asked for at or under that limit without asking again. lore pays only with what is saved in your own account on that website, and it never moves money between people.
  • Private notes in a group. After lore answers you in a group, it may send you a separate private note that draws on what you told it one to one. That note goes only to you; the group's reply never includes your private context.
  • No decisions with legal effects.Decisions lore's AI makes. lore does not use your information to make decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects. lore's AI does decide who joins lore (see "How you join"), and a person will review that decision if you ask. When lore's AI judges whether someone might fit a request, people still decide for themselves.
  • It can be wrong. AI can misunderstand or remember something incorrectly. You can ask lore how it knows something, correct it, or tell it to forget it.

Groups, one-to-one chats and what others see

  • What you say in a group is visible to the people in that group and to lore. lore keeps each group's messages and memory with that group and does not use one group's memory in another group's replies.
  • What you tell lore one to one stays between you and lore. From your one-to-one chat you can ask lore about your own group chats, for example to catch up, and lore only uses groups you are in.
  • Information from your own connected accounts, such as your inbox or calendar, is sent to you privately rather than to a group.
  • The people who run lore can access stored data when needed to support you, keep lore safe, review a joining decision you asked about, or fix a problem, and only for those purposes.

Profiles, circles and asks between lores

Your lore can ask other people's lores for help, and theirs can ask yours, but only with consent at each step.

  • Your profile is your choice. A public lore profile (a link to your page on lore) is published only when you say yes. It shows your name, your photo or initial, the line you chose, and the facts you chose to make public. You can change it or turn it off at any time. Anyone with the link can message your lore from it: your lore answers from what you made public and your free and busy times, and passes everything else to you. lore never confirms a meeting for you; you do. People under 18 cannot publish a public profile.
  • Your circle. Your circle is the people you chose to have your lore ask first when you are looking for something. lore suggests people only from those you already share a chat with, and you decide who to add. Adding someone shares nothing with them. When you join lore, people who already share a chat with you may be told you joined, with only what you made findable.
  • Favors. If someone asks their lore to ask you something ("can you cover Friday?"), lore asks you privately, and only if you use lore and the asker already knows you through lore (a shared chat, a connection on lore, or their own notes about people they know). Your answer, in your words, goes back to them.
  • Intros and finds. When someone looks for a person ("who do I know who does growth marketing?"), lore looks only at people they share a chat with or are connected to through lore, and privately asks the person it picks whether they want the intro. Nobody's contact details go to the other side until that person says yes. In a wider search, a mutual contact's lore asks them first, and only their yes reaches the person they name. lore does not introduce people under 18 to adults.
  • Fit checks use your words privately. Before you are asked about someone's request, your own lore may check the request against what you have told it and decide whether it fits you, is clearly not for you, or is unsure. If it is clearly not for you, you are not asked. Only that result, and at most one short question and answer, pass between the two lores. The reason your lore gives stays with you, and the asker's lore is told to share only what the question needs, or nothing.Your lore decides what to say back to the other lore, guided by rules to share only what the request needs and never your contact details, private notes or anything you asked it to keep private. A model makes that choice, so it can make mistakes. The reason your lore gives stays with you. We keep the content of these exchanges for 90 days, then only counts.
  • You stay in control. You can say no to any ask, and you can set standing rules for lore to turn certain asks down for you, or set a limit on how many asks reach you.

How long we keep it

  • Chats and memory: kept while you or your group use lore, so it can keep remembering, until you or your group delete them. There is no fixed deletion date for these. lore stops using some facts it learned about you in a group after about 180 days, and sooner for things you said were temporary.
